Chalk and charcoal on pixel layers (board card: chalk-charcoal-look). Branch wip/charcoal.

What this is: a new pixel-layer tool, Chalk and charcoal (Alt+Shift+T), on the soft paint engine's pixel session. Pigment catches on the
high points of a paper texture and skips the low ones. Light pressure reaches only the peaks (specks); hard pressure fills the pits;
a second pass takes hold where the first only touched the peaks. The paper is a tile held still against the page (five papers:
smooth, fine, medium, laid, rough), so strokes over each other meet the same bumps. Nine media: vine charcoal, compressed charcoal,
chalk, soft pastel, oil pastel, crayon, sketch pencil, soft pencil (6B), colour pencil.

Every medium, two themes:
  <medium>-medium-{dark,light}.png         four marks: firm stroke, light stroke, pen pressure ramp, three passes over each other
  ball-<medium>-medium-{dark,light}.png    the same medium shading a ball with hatching, so it is judged as a drawing and not test marks
  compressed-charcoal-{smooth,fine,laid,rough}-{dark,light}.png   the four marks on each other paper (medium is above)
  compare-<medium>-{dark,light}.png        Frameling's marks, the ball, and a scan of the real medium at about the same size
  overview-{dark,light}.png                all nine media's marks on one sheet
  ref/<medium>.jpg                         the reference crops used in the compare sheets

Dark theme is chalk-coloured ink on dark paper, light theme is dark ink on cream paper; the colour media keep their colour.
Strokes are real pointer events with a pretend pen (pressure), on a 400 x 400 pixel sprite, at 100% zoom.

What it was judged against, plainly: real scans, not Krita or Clip Studio. Neither is installed here and I could not get a screenshot of their
brushes, so the "same brush in Krita or Clip Studio" comparison was NOT done. The references:
  vine charcoal        Edgar Degas, jockey, charcoal on paper (public domain)
  compressed charcoal  Georges Seurat, L'Echo, conte crayon on paper (public domain)
  chalk                no reference kept: the only free scan was CC BY 2.0, and this repo is MIT (see below)
  soft pastel          Edgar Degas, Ballet Rehearsal, pastel, Pushkin Museum (public domain)
  oil pastel, crayon   no reference kept: the only free wax crayon scan was CC BY-SA 4.0, and this repo is MIT (see below)
  sketch pencil        Edvard Munch, pencil sketch of Anna Dahl, 1882 (public domain)
  soft pencil          the same sketch, the hatched jacket
  colour pencil        Gabriel de Saint-Aubin, putti, graphite and red pencil (public domain)
Every reference crop in ref/ is PUBLIC DOMAIN. The two Creative Commons ones the first pass used (a CC BY 2.0
blackboard photo and a CC BY-SA 4.0 crayon scan) were taken out along with their compare sheets, because Frameling
is MIT and a share-alike image in an MIT repository puts its own terms on anything built from it. So chalk, oil
pastel and crayon have no compare sheet; their own strips and ball shots are still here.

Reading the sheets: compressed charcoal against Seurat is the closest match (the same grain over paper tooth, tone built from pressure and
passes). Vine charcoal against Degas has the right dusty, light stroke but is speckled where the real stroke is smoky, because the grain
here is whole pixels where a scan has sub-pixel grain. Chalk reads as chalk on a board but is thinner and less streaky than the thick
real stick. Oil pastel and crayon are the weakest references (one wax crayon photo). Pencils read as pencil lines with a broken, toothy edge.
