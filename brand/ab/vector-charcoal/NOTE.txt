Vector chalk and charcoal that catch on paper (board doc: vector-charcoal). Branch wip/vector-charcoal.

What this is: the paper maths of the pixel Chalk and charcoal tool (src/dry/paper.ts and maths.ts, shared, not copied) run on a vector brush
line. The line stays its points (reshape it, restyle it, tween it). When it is drawn, dabs go along it the way the pixel tool lays them, each
as wide as the line is there and as hard as the pen pressed there, and every pixel takes pigment from that reach and the height of a paper tile
held still against the page. Light pressure leaves specks, hard pressure fills the dips, a pen ramp goes from specks to solid. The line carries
a Paper tooth effect (new, in the Effects panel), which is where its settings live. Chalk, Charcoal, Soft pastel, Oil pastel, Crayon,
Vine and Compressed charcoal and the three pencils are on it by default; Oil paint, Bristle and Dry brush keep the older tiny-shape grain.
Catch on paper (Brush options, Tip) turns it off for any chalk or charcoal tip.

Files (all in both themes):
  <brush>-{dark,light}.png          four marks: firm stroke, light stroke, pen pressure ramp, three passes over each other
  ball-<brush>-{dark,light}.png     the same brush shading a ball with hatching, so it is judged as a drawing and not test marks
  <brush>-holes-{dark,light}.png    the same marks with the OLDER tiny-shape grain (vine, compressed, chalk, soft pastel only)
  compare-<brush>-{dark,light}.png  vector marks | vector ball | (older grain) | the pixel version | the real scan
  overview-{dark,light}.png         all nine brushes' marks on one sheet
  export-png-svg-pdf.png            one toothed line as it comes out of PNG (left, magnified), SVG (middle) and PDF (right)

Judged against: the same public domain scans as the pixel version (brand/ab/charcoal/ref/): Degas jockey (vine charcoal), Seurat L'Echo
(compressed charcoal), Degas Ballet Rehearsal (soft pastel), Munch (sketch and soft pencil), Saint-Aubin (colour pencil). Chalk, oil pastel
and crayon have no scan, for the licence reason in brand/ab/charcoal/NOTE.txt, so those compare sheets have no real panel. Krita and Clip
Studio were not compared (not installed).

Verdict, plainly: it matches the pixel version the user approved ("Yes, that is charcoal") and is a different thing from the tiny-shape
version they rejected. Beside the scans it has the same limit the pixel version has: compressed charcoal against Seurat is close (grain over
paper tooth, tone from pressure and passes); vine charcoal against Degas has the dusty light stroke but is speckled where the real stroke is
smoky, because the grain is whole pixels and a scan has sub-pixel grain. A second pass over a stroke does not fill the pits the first
pass touched the way the pixel tool's does (a vector line cannot see the pigment under it), so passes add by laying different specks,
not by building. Pencils are thin lines with a toothy edge, as in the pixel version.

  (ONLY=a,b limits the brushes; HOLES=1 for the older grain)

Strokes are real pointer events with a pretend pen (pressure), on a 400 x 400 page at the same size as the pixel shots.
